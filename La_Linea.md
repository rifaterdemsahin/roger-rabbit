# La Linea ("The Line")

> A cult-classic Italian animated short series created by cartoonist Osvaldo Cavandoli, featuring a minimalist character drawn as part of a single continuous infinite line.

## Synopsis

The series follows **"Mr. Linea,"** a short-tempered character who walks along an infinite horizontal line that forms his own silhouette. As he encounters various obstacles or abrupt dead ends, he breaks the fourth wall to complain to the artist—represented by a live-action hand holding a white pencil—demanding that a solution (or a new path) be drawn for him.

---

## Production History

* **Origin (1969–1971):** Osvaldo Cavandoli pitched the concept to advertising agencies. Emilio Lagostina (owner of Lagostina pressure cookers) sponsored the initial run as commercials for the Italian show *Carosello*. The character was originally named *Agostino Lagostina*.
* **Global Expansion:** After parting ways with Lagostina sponsorship, the character was simplified to *Mr. Linea* and expanded into a standalone television series that gained worldwide popularity.
* **Voice Work:** Voiced by Carlo Bonomi using a unique, expressive gibberish language (*Grammelot*). Because there was no actual spoken language, the show easily exported internationally without needing dubbing.

## Legacy & Cultural Impact

* **International Broadcast:** Aired in over 50 countries (often used as an interstitial TV filler on channels like BBC, SVT, NRK, ABC, and KQED).
* **Music Videos:** Inspired the art style for Gigi D'Agostino’s hit videos (*"Bla Bla Bla"*, *"The Riddle"*) and Jamiroquai’s *" (Don't) Give Hate a Chance"*.
* **Television & Film:** Inspired the opening title sequence for UK's *Whose Line Is It Anyway?* (Seasons 6–10) and served as inspiration for the "Jerry" soul counselors in Pixar's *Soul* (2020).
* **Advertising:** Featured in commercial campaigns for Ford, SimPlus, Kaupthing Bank, and Viasat.
